## Title
Ancient Rome Had a Pee Tax (AD 70)

## Description
Ancient Rome really had a pee tax. Here's why - and why France named its urinals after an emperor.

Roman laundries cleaned togas with urine, because the ammonia got them clean. Emperor Vespasian (AD 69-79) taxed the urine collected from public toilets. When his son Titus complained, Vespasian held a coin from the tax to his nose and asked if it smelled. It didn't - so 'money doesn't stink' (pecunia non olet). Public urinals in France were later called 'vespasiennes'.
